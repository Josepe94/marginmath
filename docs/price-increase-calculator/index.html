How it works

  • Contribution per sale = Price − Variable cost
  • Break-even sales change = (Old contribution ÷ New contribution) − 1
  • Profit change at the same volume = Price change ÷ Old contribution

The lower your margin, the more powerful a price increase is, and the more dangerous a discount.

Worked example

You sell at $100 with $60 of variable cost, so each sale contributes $40. A 10% price increase lifts the contribution to $50: you could lose up to 20% of your sales and still earn the same profit, and if nobody leaves your profit rises by 25%.

The same maths warns about discounts: a 10% discount drops the contribution to $30, so you would need 33% more sales just to stand still.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I raise my prices?

Many businesses test 5–15% increases on new customers first. Use this calculator to see the sales drop you can tolerate, then compare it with what you actually see in the test.

Why does a small discount need so many extra sales?

Because a discount comes entirely out of your margin. If your margin is thin, a 10% discount can remove a third or more of the profit on every sale.

Does this include fixed costs?

Fixed costs don't change with a price move, so they cancel out: keeping the same total contribution keeps the same profit.

What is price elasticity?

It measures how much demand changes when the price changes. This calculator doesn't need it: it tells you the break-even elasticity, which you then compare with what you expect from your customers.
