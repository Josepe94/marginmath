Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator

Start from the income you want to keep, add your business costs and taxes, and divide by the hours you can actually bill. Most freelancers underestimate the last part.

How it works

  1. Revenue needed = (Take-home pay + Expenses) ÷ (1 − Tax rate)
  2. Billable hours = (52 − Weeks off) × Billable hours per week
  3. Hourly rate = Revenue needed ÷ Billable hours

The tax step is approximate: it applies one effective rate to everything you earn after expenses. Your real tax depends on your country and your deductions, so ask an accountant before committing to a price.

Worked example

To take home $60,000 with $6,000 of expenses and a 30% effective tax rate, you need (60,000 + 6,000) ÷ 0.70 = $94,286 of revenue. With 6 weeks off and 25 billable hours a week you have 1,150 billable hours, so your minimum rate is $82 per hour, roughly $656 per 8-hour day.

Frequently asked questions

How many billable hours does a freelancer have?

Far fewer than 40 a week. Sales, admin, invoicing and learning typically take 30–50% of working time, so 20–30 billable hours a week is a realistic range for most solo freelancers.

Should I charge hourly or per project?

Use this hourly figure as your floor. Per-project or value-based pricing can earn more once you can estimate scope well, but the quote should never work out below your minimum hourly rate.

How do I convert a salary into a freelance rate?

A common rule of thumb is to divide the annual salary by 1,000 to get a rough hourly rate, because freelancers pay their own taxes, benefits, equipment and unpaid time. This calculator does the full version.

Does this include VAT or sales tax?

No. Rates here are before VAT or sales tax, which you add on top of your invoices where it applies.
