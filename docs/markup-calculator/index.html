How it works

  • Selling price = Cost × (1 + Markup)
  • Profit = Price − Cost
  • Gross margin = Markup ÷ (1 + Markup)

Worked example

You buy an item for $40 and apply a 50% markup: the price is 40 × 1.5 = $60, profit is $20 and gross margin is 20 ÷ 60 = 33.3%. Doubling the cost (100% markup, also called keystone pricing) gives a 50% margin.

Frequently asked questions

What markup should I use?

Work backwards from the margin you need to cover overheads and profit. If your overheads eat 30% of revenue and you want 10% net profit, you need about a 40% gross margin, which is a 66.7% markup.

What is keystone pricing?

Keystone pricing means doubling the wholesale cost, a 100% markup. It produces a 50% gross margin and is a common starting point in retail.

Why is my margin lower than my markup?

Because markup is measured against the smaller number (cost) and margin against the larger one (price). For any positive profit, margin is always lower than markup.

Can markup be over 100%?

Yes. A 200% markup means the price is three times the cost. Margin, however, can never reach 100%.
