How it works

  • Contribution margin = Price − Variable cost
  • Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin
  • Break-even revenue = Break-even units × Price
  • Units for a target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ Contribution margin

Worked example

With $5,000 of monthly fixed costs, a $50 price and $20 of variable cost, each sale contributes $30. You break even at 5,000 ÷ 30 = 167 units a month ($8,350 of revenue). To make $3,000 of profit you need 267 units.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if my price is below my variable cost?

You can never break even: every extra sale loses money. Raise the price or cut the variable cost before worrying about volume.

Should I round the break-even units up?

Yes. You can't sell part of a unit, so the calculator rounds up to the next whole unit.

Is break-even analysis useful for services?

Yes. Treat one billable hour or one project as a unit, and use the direct costs of delivering it (subcontractors, tools) as the variable cost.

How can I lower my break-even point?

Three levers: cut fixed costs, raise prices, or reduce the variable cost per unit. Price usually has the biggest effect because it raises the contribution of every sale.
