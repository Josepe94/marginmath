How it works

Margin and markup describe the same profit from two different angles, which is why they are so often confused.

  • Profit = Price − Cost
  • Gross margin = Profit ÷ Price × 100
  • Markup = Profit ÷ Cost × 100
  • Price for a target margin = Cost ÷ (1 − Margin)

Margin can never reach 100% (that would mean a product that cost nothing), while markup has no upper limit.

Worked example

A product costs $40 and sells for $65. Profit is $25, gross margin is 25 ÷ 65 = 38.5% and markup is 25 ÷ 40 = 62.5%.

If you want a 50% margin on the same product, the price must be 40 ÷ (1 − 0.50) = $80, not $60. Adding 50% to cost gives a 50% markup, which is only a 33.3% margin.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin?

It depends on the industry. Retail gross margins are often in the 25–50% range, software and digital products commonly exceed 70%, and grocery or wholesale can run under 20%. Compare yourself with businesses that have a similar cost structure, not with a universal number.

Is margin the same as markup?

No. Margin divides profit by the selling price; markup divides profit by the cost. The same $25 profit on a $40 cost and $65 price is a 38.5% margin but a 62.5% markup.

Does this calculate net profit margin?

It calculates gross margin per unit: price minus the direct cost of the product. Net margin also subtracts overheads such as rent, salaries, software and taxes, so it is always lower.

How do I convert markup to margin?

Margin = Markup ÷ (1 + Markup). A 50% markup is 0.5 ÷ 1.5 = 33.3% margin. Use the markup calculator for the reverse direction.
