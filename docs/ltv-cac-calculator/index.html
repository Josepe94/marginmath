How it works

  • Customer lifetime = 1 ÷ Monthly churn
  • LTV = ARPA × Gross margin ÷ Monthly churn
  • LTV : CAC = LTV ÷ CAC
  • CAC payback = CAC ÷ (ARPA × Gross margin)

This is the simple, margin-based LTV used by most SaaS dashboards. It assumes churn stays constant and ignores expansion revenue and discounting.

Worked example

A customer pays $50 a month at an 80% gross margin, so you keep $40 a month. With 3% monthly churn the average customer stays 33 months and is worth 40 ÷ 0.03 = $1,333. If acquiring them costs $400, LTV:CAC is 3.3 : 1 and you recover the CAC in 10 months.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good LTV:CAC ratio?

3:1 is the widely quoted benchmark for a healthy subscription business. Below 1:1 you lose money on every customer; far above 5:1 can mean you are under-investing in growth.

What is a good CAC payback period?

Many SaaS investors look for payback under 12 months for small-business customers. Enterprise products often accept 18–24 months because customers stay longer.

Should LTV use revenue or gross margin?

Gross margin. Using raw revenue overstates what a customer is worth because it ignores hosting, support and payment costs.

How do I use annual churn?

Convert it to monthly first: monthly churn ≈ 1 − (1 − annual churn)^(1/12). For 30% annual churn that is about 2.9% per month.
